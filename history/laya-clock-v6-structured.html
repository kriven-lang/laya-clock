How to read the clock · V5

The outer teal ring has one fixed spoke for each tala akshara. The amber ring subdivides each akshara across the entire tala cycle: Adi tala in chatusra nadai has 8 × 4 = 32 spokes, not four spokes repeated around a separate miniature clock. The highlighted amber spoke advances one subdivision at a time. The amber reference dot marks the selected offset from true north (samam). Because an integer subdivision shift leaves a uniform lattice looking identical, the reference marker makes the phase shift visible without changing the underlying tala-aligned grid.

Select the preloaded 32-unit practice korvai to see three 8-unit phrases with four silent units between them. The last Nam is played at subdivision 32, with samam on the next subdivision. This is a constructed practice example, not a traditional attributed composition. The violet ring represents the selected repeating solkattu phrase, whose length can differ from the tala cycle. Its phrase-start offset is independent of the amber reference offset. A dot in the phrase text represents one silent subdivision, not necessarily a sustained stroke. The tempo control measures tala aksharas per minute; the speed multiplier increases pulses per akshara and does not itself redefine kalai. Audio remains synthesized clicks. These motifs are illustrative practice patterns, not verified transcriptions.
